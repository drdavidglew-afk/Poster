Roots of QM and GR

Foundations · deduction map

From a set and an influence relation to quantum theory and general relativity

Five starting assumptions, five lines of deduction, and the published results that show how far each line goes. Line style marks what is a theorem, what holds only under extra hypotheses, and what is still open.

How firm is each step

● FIRM A published theorem under the stated axioms.
◐ CONDITIONAL Needs an extra hypothesis, holds only in a limit, in expectation, or at linear order.
○ OPEN Conjectured, obstructed, or no derivation known.
Thin grey lines link an assumption to the lanes that use it. Hover an assumption to trace them.

Starting assumptions

A1Events and a may-influence relation

A set X. For each x, a subset J(x) of events that could influence it. Closing under transitivity gives a preorder.

A2Influence has a speed limit

One invariant finite speed c. A relativity principle allows only c = ∞ (Galilean) or finite c (Lorentz): Ignatowsky 1910, Lévy-Leblond 1976.

A3Reversible, composable processes

Processes compose in sequence and in parallel, and every process has an inverse (a dagger).

A4Local finiteness

A bounded causal interval holds finitely many events. The number of events measures volume.

A5Probabilistic outcomes, no signalling

Processes give probabilities. Discarding the output of a later process cannot change an earlier one. Mixed states have purifications.

Lines of deduction

Causal route

toward GRfrom A1A2A4

Order plus number gives geometry. The weak link is the dynamics.

Causal order

x ≺ y when x may influence y. A locally finite partial order, a causal set.

● FIRM

Order ⇒ conformal metric

Zeeman 1964; Hawking–King–McCarthy 1976; Malament 1977

For orders that come from a spacetime. Zeeman needs dimension ≥ 3.

Conformal class [g]

Light cones are fixed. The metric is known up to a position-dependent factor Ω².

◐ CONDITIONAL

Counting ⇒ volume ⇒ full metric

Bombelli–Lee–Meyer–Sorkin 1987; Myrheim 1978; Bombelli–Henson–Sorkin 2009

Needs a faithful embedding of the discrete order in a manifold (Hauptvermutung, unproved). Lorentz-invariant discreteness must be a random sprinkling.

Lorentzian metric g

Order plus number gives volume, dimension and curvature estimators.

◐ CONDITIONAL

Metric ⇒ action ⇒ Einstein–Hilbert

Benincasa–Dowker 2010; Dowker–Glaser 2013; Kleitman–Rothschild 1975

Holds in expectation on sprinklings. Almost all large posets are not manifold-like, so dynamics must select the geometry.

Discrete classical gravity

The BDG action approximates ∫R√−g. Dimension 3+1 and Lorentz invariance are not selected.

Orthogonality route

toward QM and GRfrom A1A3

Complement is a shared construction. The two lattices part ways at orthomodularity.

Orthogonality relation

x ⊥ y when x and y are incomparable (spacelike). Symmetric and irreflexive. On the Hilbert side, ⟨x|y⟩ = 0.

● FIRM

⊥⊥-closure ⇒ orthocomplemented lattice

Birkhoff–von Neumann 1936

Pure order theory (a Galois connection). ⊥ is symmetric, so it forgets the arrow of time.

Complement lattice

Closed sets S = S⊥⊥. Hilbert space: closed subspaces. Spacetime: causally closed regions. Both are non-distributive.

◐ CONDITIONAL

Orthomodular, atomic, covering law ⇒ Hilbert space

Piron 1964; Solèr 1995; Casini 2002

A theorem only for orthomodular lattices. Causal lattices are orthocomplemented but not orthomodular (Casini).

Hilbert space

Over R, C or H. The lattice does not single out the complex numbers.

● FIRM○ OPEN · causal side

Measure on subspaces ⇒ Born rule

Gleason 1957; Sorkin 1994

Gleason needs dimension ≥ 3. Sorkin's quantum measure postulates the rule for causal sets; no Gleason-type theorem is known there.

Born rule

P(x) = |⟨x|ψ⟩|². Overlap is graded here, while causal ⊥ is binary. Graded causality is unresolved.

Process route

toward QMfrom A3A5

Composition and causality alone give the shape of quantum theory. Spacetime topology has the same shape; the metric is absent.

Symmetric monoidal dagger category

Systems are objects, processes are morphisms. ⊗ is parallel composition, † reverses a process.

● FIRM◐ duals assumed

Add duals (cups, caps) ⇒ dagger compact

Abramsky–Coecke 2004; Selinger 2007; Baez–Stay 2011

Compact closure is an input. Infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces are not compact closed.

Dagger compact category

Models: finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, and cobordisms (Atiyah 1988; Lurie 2009). Diagram equalities hold iff diagrams are isotopic. Cobordisms carry topology only.

● FIRM

Causality + purification + more ⇒ finite-dimensional QM

Hardy 2001; Chiribella–D'Ariano–Perinotti 2011; Selinger 2007

The postulates carry the content; purification is the key one.

Finite quantum theory

Complex Hilbert spaces, unitaries, tensor product, entanglement, Born rule.

◐ CONDITIONAL

Infinitely many degrees of freedom ⇒ nets of algebras

Haag 1992; Doplicher–Roberts 1990

Presupposes the spacetime that was to be derived. Circular for our purpose.

Local quantum fields

Sectors form a rigid C*-tensor category and a compact gauge group G is recovered. Spacetime is an input.

Continues in the algebraic bridge.

Algebraic bridge

where QM meets GRfrom A1A2A3

Modular flow supplies boosts. Entanglement supplies Einstein's equation, once an area-entropy law is assumed.

Net of algebras

Each region O carries an algebra 𝒜(O). Isotony, microcausality, Haag duality 𝒜(O′) = 𝒜(O)′, and a state ω.

● FIRM○ OPEN · in general

State ⇒ modular flow = boosts, J = CPT

Takesaki 1970; Bisognano–Wichmann 1975; Brunetti–Guido–Longo 2002; Borchers 2000

Theorem for wedges in QFT. Modular flow as physical time in general is the thermal time hypothesis (Connes–Rovelli 1994), a conjecture.

Symmetry from algebras

The Poincaré group and CPT are recovered from the modular data of wedge algebras.

◐ CONDITIONAL

First law δS = δ⟨K⟩ plus S = A/4G ⇒ Einstein equation

Jacobson 1995, 2016; Lashkari–McDermott–Van Raamsdonk 2014; Faulkner et al. 2014

Linearised order is rigorous in holographic CFTs. Nonlinear order needs Jacobson's assumptions. A/4G is an input.

Einstein equation from entanglement

Horizon entropy plays the role of entanglement across the region. Gab + Λgab = 8πG Tab follows.

◐ CONDITIONAL

Include gravity ⇒ type III becomes type II

Witten 2018; Chandrasekaran–Longo–Penington–Witten 2022

Shown semiclassically for the de Sitter static patch with an observer.

Gravitational algebra

Entropy is finite and a trace exists. The observer and clock belong to the system.

Dynamical cone route

toward quantum gravityfrom A2A3

A light cone is easy when the wiring is fixed. Letting matter move the wiring is the unsolved step.

Quantum cellular automaton

Cells on a fixed graph. Influence moves one edge per tick, so c = edge/tick.

● FIRM

Locality + unitarity ⇒ light cone

Lieb–Robinson 1972; Schumacher–Werner 2004; Bialynicki-Birula 1994

The inverse of a reversible QCA is a QCA, so † is built in.

Exact causal cone

Microcausality is exact and Haag duality holds on finite lattices. The Dirac equation emerges in the continuum limit. Lorentz invariance is approximate.

○ OPEN

Let the graph depend on matter

Arrighi–Martiel 2017; Hardy 2005; Oreshkov–Costa–Brukner 2012

Unitary, causal dynamics on changing graphs exists as a framework. None is coupled to energy to give Einstein's equation.

Superposed cones

|Ψ⟩ = ΣG αG |G⟩ ⊗ |cells on G⟩. Distance is an operator, observables must be relational, H|Ψ⟩ = 0.

Giacomini–Castro-Ruiz–Brukner 2019; Höhn–Smith–Lock 2021

◐ CONDITIONAL○ OPEN · dS

Partial answers: CDT and holography

Ambjørn–Jurkiewicz–Loll 2004; Maldacena 1997; Ryu–Takayanagi 2006; Gao–Wald 2000

CDT is numerical. Holography needs an AdS boundary, and our universe has Λ > 0.

Quantum gravity: unsolved

Needs a Hilbert space that includes the wiring, unitary evolution with a per-branch causal bound, relational observables, and wiring that responds to energy.

● Firm

  • Causal order fixes the conformal metric (Zeeman; Hawking–King–McCarthy; Malament).
  • ⊥⊥-closure gives an orthocomplemented lattice.
  • Gleason: the Born rule on a Hilbert lattice of dimension ≥ 3.
  • Finite-dimensional QM from causality and purification postulates.
  • Wedge modular flow is the boost (Bisognano–Wichmann).
  • Locality plus unitarity gives a light cone (Lieb–Robinson).

◐ Conditional

  • Counting gives a metric only if discrete orders embed faithfully in manifolds.
  • The BDG action matches Einstein–Hilbert in expectation only.
  • Lattice-to-Hilbert theorems need orthomodularity, which causal lattices lack.
  • Einstein's equation from entanglement: linear order, or with Jacobson's assumptions.
  • Holographic derivations need an AdS boundary.
  • Compact closure is assumed, and algebraic QFT presupposes spacetime.

○ Open

  • A Gleason-type theorem for causal lattices, and graded causality.
  • Why complex numbers, and why 3+1 dimensions.
  • Selecting manifold-like orders dynamically (Kleitman–Rothschild problem).
  • Energy-coupled dynamics of the causal cone with an Einstein-equation limit.
  • A de Sitter or closed-universe Hilbert space.
